## Quality Standards (User's Explicit Requirements)

The user has articulated specific quality standards for this class of work. These are NON-NEGOTIABLE:

1. **第一性原则 (First Principles)**: Ground analysis in root causes and fundamental truths, not surface trends. Question every assumption. The core question: "What is the user/customer actually buying, and why?"

2. **可实践性 (Actionable)**: Every recommendation must include specific "how" — tools, team size, timelines, budget ranges, SOPs. No vague "consider doing X." If you recommend "AI content production," specify which tools, what workflow, expected output per day.

3. **严谨 (Rigorous)**: Logical chain must hold. Every strategy recommendation must trace back to a diagnosed pain point or opportunity from the source materials. No unsupported claims.

4. **务实 (Practical)**: Recommendations must fit the user's actual resource constraints (budget, team size, timeline). If they have a 3-person team, don't propose a 20-person organizational structure. If they have zero ad budget, don't lead with paid acquisition.

5. **有数据支持 (Data-Supported)**: Use industry benchmarks, conversion rate data, pricing references, market size figures. When exact data isn't available, use reasonable estimates clearly marked as "约" (approximately) or "参考" (reference). Never fabricate precise numbers.

6. **能拿到结果 (Results-Oriented)**: Every chapter should have measurable outcomes. Include KPI dashboards, milestone checkpoints, and success/failure criteria.

## Document Structure Pattern

For multi-dimensional business analysis, use this proven structure:

```
1. Market Overview & Data Anchors (宏观数据锚点)
2-12. Full-chain dimension-by-dimension analysis
13. Execution Roadmap + KPIs + Budget
```

Each dimension chapter should contain:
- **Diagnosis**: What's the current state / problem from source materials
- **Strategy**: What to do about it
- **Tactics**: Specific actions, tools, workflows
- **Metrics**: How to measure success
- **Pitfalls**: What could go wrong and how to mitigate

## Research Workflow

When the user provides source materials (meeting notes, transcripts):

1. **Deep-read all attachments first** — Extract all strategic decisions, pain points, constraints, and data points. These are your primary source.

2. **Attempt web research for supporting data** — Market size, industry benchmarks, competitor examples, case studies. Use browser search or terminal curl. BUT: in WSL environments, Bing China often returns garbage results for Chinese business queries, and Google/DuckDuckGo may timeout behind proxies.

3. **Pivot to training knowledge when search fails** — If 2-3 search attempts yield nothing useful, STOP searching. Your training data contains substantial industry knowledge. Clearly mark training-knowledge data as "约" or "参考" rather than cited sources. The quality of your domain knowledge + the user's source materials is sufficient for a high-quality plan.

4. **Never spend >5 minutes on research dead ends** — If searches are not productive, pivot immediately to writing. The user's deadline matters more than perfect citations.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Over-relying on search for data validation** — In Chinese business contexts, web search often fails due to proxy/network issues. Trust your training knowledge when searches fail. Mark estimates honestly.

2. **Making the plan too abstract** — "Build a brand" is useless advice. "Post 3 Douyin videos/day targeting 25-35yo parents, measure CTR after 30 days, aim for >3% engagement rate" is actionable.

3. **Ignoring resource constraints** — Always ask: "Can they actually do this with their current team/budget?" If not, phase it.

4. **Forgetting the "first principles" test** — After writing each section, ask: "Would this still be true if all the industry jargon were stripped away?"

5. **Over-structuring** — Not every section needs sub-sections. If a dimension only needs 2 paragraphs and a table, that's fine. Don't inflate to fill space.
